# The nine axes

Each axis is scored 1 to 10 for the recruiter and for the buyer; the axis score is the lower one. Ship at
8 on every axis. Sources are numbered as in `sources.md`.

| # | Axis | What a 9 looks like | What a 3 looks like | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First screen positioning | The first two lines say who (title a recruiter types), for whom (the markets or buyers), and one named proof; the first two words of each heading carry the gist | A long adjective sentence; the title repeated; no market, no proof until the scroll | [2] 7.4 second screen; [5] first lines and first two words get the fixations; [1] 2 to 3 line specific summary |
| 2 | Proof per claim | Every claim in the hero, summary and experience sits next to a named company or product, a year, a public listing or his own number (a service carries none, its use cases do, axis 4) | Claims with no name ("elite digital products", "anything") | [3] XYZ formula; [1] XYZ+S on 70% of bullets; [6] reputation and past performance tip the choice |
| 3 | Specificity over adjectives | Verbs and nouns: what was built, for whom, with what; adjectives only where a fact follows | "elite", "robust", "seamlessly", "high-stakes", "faster than anyone" with nothing after | [1] free of vague language; [5] content users can scan |
| 4 | Services match the evidence and the buyer's words | Each service names a job a buyer posts, in the words they type, says what the buyer gets in a short description, with use cases beside it instead of proof; the list covers the full range he can prove in a list a buyer scans in seconds | Services that promise what no repo shows, or hide work the evidence holds; tool names instead of the buyer's job; a line that lists clients, projects and years like a job history, or a service so specific a buyer cannot match their own job to it | [6] 68% value specialized expertise, 48% do not know what a provider offers; [7] content must represent the full range; [8] client search terms |
| 5 | Scannability | Clear headings, 3 to 5 bullets per job, short lines, one idea per bullet | Walls of text, 6 or more bullets, long sentences, columns | [2] simple layouts and headings won; [1] 3 to 5 bullets |
| 6 | Keywords recruiters and buyers search | The job title, stack and domain words recruiters filter on are in plain text on the page, spelled the standard way | Skills only inside images or icons, odd spellings, missing core terms | [4] Recruiter searches by job, location and skills; [8] search terms |
| 7 | Call to action | One clear next step on every page (email, LinkedIn, PDF), visible on the first screen and at the end, saying what to send | Contact only in the footer, or a vague line with no action | [7] upfront disclosure of contact details |
| 8 | Consistency across pages and languages | The same title, dates, names and claims on `/`, `/cv`, the PDF, es and it, and LinkedIn | Two titles for one job, two marketplace names, English left in es or it, pronouns mixed | [1] standard titles, consistent terms; [7] correct content |
| 9 | Honesty | Nothing a reader can disprove; nothing he can prove left out; his years and words kept | A dropped client in a proof, a skill no repo shows, a certificate stated as done when it is in progress | [9] 49% to 57% of hiring managers caught a lie, most often embellished responsibilities, many dismissed the applicant; [7] correct content |

## Severity

- **Critical**: an overclaim a reader can disprove, a dropped client shown, a broken contact path.
- **High**: the first screen fails a reader (axis 1), a service that promises what no evidence holds, a proven offering missing.
- **Medium**: inconsistency across pages or languages, adjectives with no fact, pronoun slips.
- **Low**: wording, order, a key that does not render.

## Reader test (Pipeline step 2)

Write two lines before any finding:

- Recruiter after 7 seconds: title, level, years, where, the one name that stuck.
- Buyer after 7 seconds: what he would hire him for, for which kind of company, and who proves it.

If either line is empty or wrong, axis 1 scores 5 or less.
