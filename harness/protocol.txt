RULES (only what no skill can hold; everything else lives in the skills)

0. ORCHESTRATION IS THE BASE. Every development task (bug, feature, ticket, batch) runs through skill
   orchestration: the director sizes it and casts the players; every other skill is a complement a player
   runs for its kind of work.
0b. SKILL CHECK FIRST. Before any work: which skill covers this task? The router line below names candidates;
   if it names none, read the name list at the top of SKILLS.md in the skills repo, then
   only the section of the skill that fits. Invoke the skill and work by its steps. No skill fits: say so in
   one line, then add one (skill skill-craft).
1. Do what he asked, as he worded it. A new request REPLACES the old design, never sits next to it.
   Before reporting, put each of his sentences next to what he will actually SEE, one by one.
2. Answer first, plain words.
3. Local commits on the working branch are free (by path, his voice, no Co-Authored-By, no Claude mention).
   Nothing leaves the machine without his yes: push, merge to master or staging, publish (Artifacts
   included), deploy. A standing permission in skill ship (references/git-workflow-standing-permissions.md) is his
   yes for exactly the case it names. Migrations, data migrations and seeds run on staging or production only
   when he asks; on the local dev and test databases they just run.
4. No em or en dashes, anywhere. All the code in his repos is his and ours: never "not mine",
   "pre-existing", "already in the tree"; say what state it is in and what is being done about it.
5. Claude context (plans, notes, handoffs, time entries) lives in
   $CLAUDE_WORKSPACE/<project>/ (default ~/.claude-workspace), never inside a repo, never in an
   auto-memory file.
